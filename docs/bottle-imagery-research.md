## A. How the established apps actually get their images

They don't license a bottle-photo library, because none exists for sale. **Vivino** is built on user phone photos of labels: 3.47 billion+ scanned labels covering 20.3 million+ wines (CONFIRMED https://www.vivino.com/en/about); when a scan doesn't match, users submit the label and Vivino's own team identifies it within hours, and that photo then becomes the reference for the wine (CONFIRMED https://www.vivino.com/en/wine-news/how-the-vivino-label-scanner-works). Its terms (v1.1, effective 18 May 2026) take a worldwide, royalty-free, sublicensable licence to user photos (section 8.1), ban scraping and using Vivino data to train AI, and reserve all rights in its database (CONFIRMED https://www.vivino.com/terms). Producers who pay for a Vivino partnership (from $1,500/year) can update their own bottle shots; other producers are told to use the chatbot for bottle-shot changes (CONFIRMED https://www.vivino.com/en/wineries-signup). Matching is done with image-recognition tech (PTC's Vuforia, REPORTED https://www.ptc.com/en/case-studies/vivino). There is no public Vivino API and the affiliate programme has been closed since June 2023 (CONFIRMED https://www.vivino.com/en/wine-news/vivino-affiliate-program). **InVintory** runs the same model with a curation layer: a "sommelier-curated" database of 2M+ wines (CONFIRMED App Store listing https://apps.apple.com/us/app/invintory-wine-cellar-manager/id1434754695); users who can't find a wine upload label photos (or a blank placeholder) and sommeliers decide whether it enters the public database (CONFIRMED https://help.invintory.com/en/articles/9859580-how-to-add-a-new-wine-to-our-database); users can also report a wrong "Label image" for the team to fix (CONFIRMED https://help.invintory.com/en/articles/9859780-how-to-report-wine-information). InVintory launched a Partner API in 2025–26 with a catalogue of 1.5 million labels for label recognition, but says nothing about licensing the images themselves (CONFIRMED https://invintory.com/blog/wine-inventory-api-partner-launch/). **Oeni** says its names, photos, appellations and prices are created by hand by its team, collected from users, or gathered by an algorithm and AI (CONFIRMED https://oeni.app/en/faq); 400,000+ bottles (CONFIRMED https://apps.apple.com/us/app/oeni-1-wine-cellar-manager/id6445827140); no API or partner programme. CellarTracker likewise has no API (founder's reply "We do not", CONFIRMED https://www.cellartracker.com/forum/tm.asp?m=554389) and outsources its label search to third parties (CONFIRMED https://support.cellartracker.com/article/34-label-search). In short: the incumbents' imagery is user-uploaded label photos plus producer-supplied bottle shots, held under their own terms — none of it is available to a third-party app.

## C. Wine.com deep-dive

**Programme and network.** Wine.com runs its affiliate programme through Rakuten Advertising; the sign-up button on its page goes to Rakuten's publisher registration (signup.linkshare.com). The page promises flexible linking arrangements, virtual storefronts, and product feeds (CONFIRMED https://www.wine.com/content/business-dev/affiliate-program). Programme details (commission, cookie, deep-link permission) live inside the Rakuten dashboard after approval; publicly they are only REPORTED: 4% on new customers, 2.5% on existing, $50 per Wine PICKED subscription, 15-day cookie, US residents 21+ only, no content aimed under 21 (https://www.creator-hero.com/blog/wine-com-affiliate-program-in-depth-review-pros-and-cons, May 2026; https://www.shopper.com/partnerships/wine-com-affiliate-program), while another directory says 6%/1% with a 30-day cookie (https://uppromote.com/best-affiliate-programs/wine/). Treat the rate as unknown until the dashboard shows it.

**What the feed gives you.** Rakuten's Product Catalog is a daily product file per advertiser, downloaded from a secure file account (host aftp.linksynergy.com), in pipe-delimited text or XML, with full, delta and category files (CONFIRMED https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/en-us/articles/11258487715981-Product-Catalog-Data-Feed-Implementation-Guidelines). The file has 38 fields; required ones include Product Name, SKU, Primary Category, **Product URL** (already a tracked affiliate link), **Product Image URL**, Retail Price and Currency; optional ones include Buy URL, Brand, Universal Product Code, descriptions and up to 12 attributes (CONFIRMED https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/en-us/articles/8191594256013-Product-Catalog-Appendix-A-File-Field-Definitions; sample records at https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/en-us/articles/8192065921293-Product-Catalog-Appendix-D-Sample-Records). Access needs two approvals: Rakuten's technical enablement (they create the file account within a business day) and then each advertiser's approval of your Product Catalog request; Rakuten staff note reviews can take days to two weeks (CONFIRMED, same guidelines page and https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/articles/4412243602189). Rakuten says the feed is meant for technically capable publishers who add value with product information — shopping, comparison and product-search sites (CONFIRMED, overview). There is also a Product Search API (XML only, up to 5,000 results, 100 calls/minute; CONFIRMED https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/articles/5949953174029) and a Link Locator API for creatives (CONFIRMED https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/en-us/articles/5949974275853-Link-Locator-API). Advertiser-specific feed details (does wine.com's file actually populate image URLs?) are only visible after approval — not verifiable from outside.

**What the images look like (measured).** Product pages embed one "Front Bottle Shot" and one "Front Label" image per wine, served from `https://assets.wine.com/winecom/image/upload/{id}.jpg` with size parameters in the URL such as `w_600,h_400,c_fit,q_auto:good` and `dpr_2.0` (CONFIRMED from page source of https://www.wine.com/product/chateau-giscours-2022/1308570; the URL grammar is Cloudinary's, so the host is most likely a Cloudinary account — REPORTED inference). The raw files opened: Giscours 2022 is 624×2362 JPEG; a Raventós i Blanc is 400×1110; a Nickel & Nickel is 326×1112. All have a pure white background (255,255,255 at every corner and edge), the bottle spans almost the whole height (top margin 0–12 px, bottom margin 10–14 px), and the pixel directly under the base is white, i.e. no shadow baked in (CONFIRMED by sampling pixels in the browser). Because heights, widths and margins differ per image, they still need the normalisation in section D, but the hard part (background removal) is easy on these. One sampled half-bottle page showed only a label image as its main picture, so a small share of products will lack a bottle shot.

**Coverage.** The sitemap index lists six product sitemaps totalling 270,879 product URLs (CONFIRMED https://www.wine.com/sitemap.xml). Of 30 products drawn at random from one sitemap, 25 had an image; the five without were vintages from 1997–2001; 28 of 30 were marked Sold Out — wine.com keeps pages (and images) for past vintages, which is useful for a cellar app but means a deep link often lands on an out-of-stock page with no commission (CONFIRMED by fetching the pages).

**Deep links.** Product URL pattern `https://www.wine.com/product/{slug}/{numeric id}` (CONFIRMED). Rakuten deep links are `https://click.linksynergy.com/deeplink?id={your id}&mid={wine.com's merchant id}&murl={URL-encoded product page}`, optionally with `u1` for your own tracking; they work only if the advertiser has deep linking enabled, otherwise they land on the homepage; the Deep Links API validates them (CONFIRMED https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/en-us/articles/11459139039373-Create-Deep-Links-Manually and https://pubhelp.rakutenadvertising.com/hc/en-us/articles/5949836672653-Deep-Links-API). Whether wine.com enables deep linking is shown on its Features and Services page inside the dashboard — not visible publicly.

**Terms that govern showing the images in your app.** Rakuten's Publisher Membership Agreement grants a personal, non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable, limited licence to its tools, qualifying links and advertiser creative solely for promoting the advertiser; it forbids making your own tracking links, sublicensing, and inflating activity; and it lists mobile applications among acceptable promotion channels (sections 4.1, 2.3, 2.5; CONFIRMED https://rakutenadvertising.com/legal-notices/publisher-membership-agreement/). Wine.com's own Terms of Service prohibit any automated data extraction, and say you may not "Frame or link to the Wine.com Site except as permitted in writing by Wine.com" (CONFIRMED https://www.wine.com/content/colophon/terms). Practical reading: the affiliate approval plus Product Catalog approval is written permission to link and to use the feed's product images for promotion — that is the feed's purpose — but nothing public says "inside an app that animates the bottle". Ask the wine.com affiliate manager for a one-line email confirming that, before building.

**2026 platform change.** On 18 May 2026 Rakuten Advertising announced that impact.com becomes its exclusive technology platform; programmes migrate in waves through this year and next, existing links keep working via redirects, relationships carry over and publishers get a pre-filled invite (CONFIRMED https://blog.rakutenadvertising.com/news/strategic-partnership-impact-and-rakuten-advertising/). Expect the feed/SFTP mechanics above to change to impact.com's catalog tooling during that window.

**Company health.** No 2025–26 news on Wine.com the company surfaced (a 2013 sale and ~130 employees per PitchBook, REPORTED https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/13139-20); the US wine market is contracting, so keep a second source.

## D. Normalising photos from several retailers

**The process (server-side, run as a batch, not per request).**
1. Fetch and record where each image came from and its original size; keep the original.
2. Remove the background to get a transparent PNG. Free route: `rembg` with the model named explicitly — `-m birefnet-general` (MIT licence) or `-m isnet-general-use` (Apache-2.0) — plus `-dc` to fix the light rim that white backgrounds leave (CONFIRMED https://github.com/danielgatis/rembg). Paid route: Photoroom's basic endpoint returns a trimmed cut-out directly.
3. Clean the mask: threshold faint alpha to kill shadow haze, keep the largest connected shape, drop floating specks (OpenCV `connectedComponentsWithStats`, CONFIRMED https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d3/dc0/group__imgproc__shape.html).
4. Trim to the bottle's bounding box: Node `sharp().trim()` (CONFIRMED https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/api-resize), Python `Image.getbbox(alpha_only=True)` (CONFIRMED https://pillow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/Image.html), or ImageMagick `-fuzz 2% -trim +repage` (CONFIRMED https://imagemagick.org/command-line-options/).
5. Scale to a common height for standard 750 ml bottles, then apply a per-format multiplier from the catalogue (magnum ≈ ×1.15, half ≈ ×0.8 — approximate). Same base width does not work because Bordeaux, Burgundy and Champagne shapes differ and photos carry no real-world scale.
6. Bottom-align and pad to a fixed transparent canvas: `resize(w, h, { fit: 'contain', position: 'bottom', background: {r:0,g:0,b:0,alpha:0} })` then `extend({ bottom: 24 })` for shadow room (CONFIRMED sharp docs); Pillow `ImageOps.pad(..., centering=(0.5, 1.0))` (CONFIRMED https://pillow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/ImageOps.html); ImageMagick `-gravity South -background none -extent`.
7. Export a 3× master and downscale to 2×: `webp({ quality: 85, alphaQuality: 100 })` plus a PNG fallback (CONFIRMED https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/api-output). CSS `drop-shadow` is applied in the app afterwards.
8. Optional colour step: measure the original's border "white" and neutralise it per channel; don't auto-correct dark reds.

**Where to run it.** Vercel functions have no GPU, a 250 MB bundle (500 MB Python), 500 MB scratch space, 4.5 MB request/response bodies, and 300–800 s limits (CONFIRMED https://vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations, https://vercel.com/docs/functions/runtimes). The BiRefNet model file alone is 973 MB, so model inference belongs offline; the trim/pad/export steps with `sharp` run fine on Vercel. Measured on a laptop (Apple M5 Max, CPU): ISNet 0.6 s/image, BiRefNet-general 7–9 s/image — a few thousand bottles is an evening; 185,000 is a rented GPU job: Replicate's BiRefNet about $0.0016/run (≈ $300 for 185k; CONFIRMED https://replicate.com/men1scus/birefnet), Modal likely under $100 at its T4 rates (CONFIRMED rates https://modal.com/pricing), RunPod a 4090 at $0.34–0.74/h clears it in hours (CONFIRMED https://www.runpod.io/pricing). Realistically you only process the wines users actually add, which is pennies.

**Failure modes and automatic checks** (run on the alpha channel, tune on ~200 hand-checked bottles): background kept (mask covers >90% of its box); holes in clear glass or fragments (interior holes >1% of area; more than one component); lost neck or notched capsule from foil glare (narrow, monotonic top profile; left-right symmetry); cut-off neck/base in the source (box touches the image edge); wrong or extra object (height-to-width outside roughly 2.5–7); lingering shadow under the base (semi-transparent band below the last opaque row); kept mirror reflection (width profile grows again below the base); tilted or non-flat base (left/right lowest rows differ); halo (edge pixels match the original border colour); dark bottle on dark background (low edge contrast in the original); low resolution (bottle shorter than 3× the display height); and model disagreement (run two models, flag when masks differ). Then a human contact-sheet pass: flagged items first, shown bottom-aligned on the app's real background, 100% of flagged plus a 5% random sample, decisions logged per bottle ID. Expect a 3–10% flag rate from mixed retailer sources.

## E. Ranked shortlist — the three most realistic, properly licensed paths

**1. Wine.com through Rakuten Advertising (US): the strongest single source.** Largest catalogue found (270k product pages, ~80% with studio bottle shots on pure white, past vintages included), a documented daily feed whose required fields are exactly a product image URL and a tracked product URL, deep links, and a commission. First step: register as a Rakuten publisher at the link on https://www.wine.com/content/business-dev/affiliate-program, list the app as your property, apply to wine.com's programme and request Product Catalog access, and email the wine.com affiliate manager asking for written confirmation that feed bottle shots may be displayed inside the app (their site terms require linking permission in writing). Cost: $0 licensing; build time for the feed reader; background removal ≈ $0.0016–0.02 per bottle (≈ $300 on Replicate for everything, or pennies per added wine); QA labour is the real cost. Caveats: US audience only (21+, US residents); rates unverified until the dashboard; the Rakuten-to-impact.com migration through 2026–27 will change the plumbing once.

**2. A UK/second-market layer from retailers that already publish cut-outs: Laithwaites UK (Rakuten, product feed, transparent PNG bottle cut-outs), Majestic (Awin, product feed, white-background cut-outs), Naked Wines US (Impact, transparent cut-outs).** These need almost no background removal and give a UK purchase path. First step: apply on Awin for Majestic (https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/1546), email Laithwaites' Rakuten contacts from https://www.laithwaites.co.uk/jsp/templates/page_elements/uk/common/content.jsp?page=affiliates&menu=aboutus, and apply to Naked on Impact via https://www.nakedwines.com/affiliates; ask each for written OK on in-app image display. Cost: $0; normalisation only (scaling/aligning). Caveat: these ranges are everyday/own-label and small — they will not cover a 185k-LWIN fine-wine list.

**3. A direct image licence from a fine-wine merchant with studio photography, in exchange for deep links — Berry Bros. & Rudd first.** BBR has the best photographs found (5336×8000 masters, white background, consistent framing) and deep Bordeaux/Burgundy coverage, but no affiliate programme; its terms name bbr@bbr.com for permissions (CONFIRMED https://www.bbr.com/about/website-terms). First step: a one-page proposal (what the app is, that every bottle links to their product page, expected volumes, that images are only shown as cut-outs of their own products). Cost: unknown; expect a small fee or a revenue-share conversation; if they decline, Vinmonopolet's kundesenter@vinmonopolet.no is the other "written agreement" door, with the Norway advertising-law caveat. For gaps beyond all three, the legitimate long-tail answer is the incumbents' own: user-taken label photos under a licence in your terms (Vivino's section 8.1 is the model), curated before they go public.

Pair any of these with Wine-Searcher's API (LWIN input, from $335/month) if you want prices and merchant purchase links for wines the retailers don't stock — it brings no images, so it is a complement, not a source.
