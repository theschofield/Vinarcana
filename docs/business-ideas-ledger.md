## Positioning note (Ed's framing, keep for the plan's market section)

Vivino is the foil: their bet is crowd-sourced transparency per bottle; Ed's bet is that
information doesn't create caring. "Picking wine is hard — let the spirits do it."
Curation + gacha mechanics + the profound feeling of being understood → desire to learn.
Not mutually exclusive with Vivino, but philosophically opposite ("race to blandness").
Because the mechanism is *trust the suggestion*, featured placement is natural inventory.

## Idea 1 — Regional physical decks + featured-slot partnerships

**As told:** Physical tarot decks scoped to a place (wine region, country, maybe a
specific bar), made with commissioned artists, sold as collectible souvenirs in tasting
rooms, gift stores, wine-region souvenir shops. Each deck: own artwork, own wine subset,
reskins the app's look. Cards are scannable — physical reading → scan → Lenses +
guidebook in app. B2B layer: wineries pay/invest to be the featured recommendation on a
card ("proudly the main recommendation for Wheel of Fortune"). Tourism boards as
possible buyers/sponsors of a region deck they'd be proud of. Flagship scene: tourist in
a Napa tasting room buys the deck the winery sells because the winery is featured in it.

**Revenue streams inside this idea (separate buyers, separate economics):**
1. Deck retail/wholesale margin (consumer buys a $35–45 collectible).
2. Featured-slot sponsorship — wineries pay flat fees to be in the deck (Ed's "each
   winery is an investor" instinct; strongest form: presell slots to fund the print run).
3. Tourism-board sponsorship/licensing of a whole region deck (slow sales cycle; later).
4. Wineries as channel: buy wholesale, retail in tasting room at margin.

**Money instincts (Ed, unpressed):** sell/license the slot; maybe cut of sales; maybe
activation fee; maybe deck rev-share with featured wineries. Unsure of winery
willingness to pay — wine industry is odd (free pours to drive discovery).

**Tone/risk answer:** regional decks can slide the tone scale (dense esoteric → slimmed
oracle, non-tarot symbols) to embody the place; first partners closest to the original
tone. Tarot is culturally ascendant, gift-shop-mainstream already.

**⚠ Legal flag (needs alcohol-beverage attorney before any deal):** California
tied-house laws + third-party-marketing rules. Rule of thumb pending counsel: flat
sponsorship fees = advertising (likely fine, FTC disclosure required); percentage of
*wine* sales as an unlicensed party = danger zone. Rev-share on *deck* sales is fine —
decks aren't alcohol.

**Decisions (Jul 15):**
- **CORE TENET (ratified):** paid placement lives only in scoped decks; the main
  app's spirits are never for sale.
- **Michelin twist (Ed):** featured slots in a deck are *editorially awarded*, not
  sold — "we reach out and let them know we're featuring them"; the prestige is the
  hook. Working hybrid to preserve revenue: the award is free and non-negotiable;
  featured wineries are then offered a **launch-partner package** — a wholesale
  precommitment (e.g. ~50 decks) + launch activation — so money buys inventory and
  marketing, never the placement. 22 wineries × ~50 decks × ~$30 wholesale ≈ $33k
  precommitted ≈ funds the edition. Pure-editorial fallback: Ed fronts ~$15–25k.
- **First edition: Sonoma** ("it's the cooler one"), 22 Major Arcana only; 78 later
  only if demand forces it.
- **Price point: $60+ art object**, not a churned-out deck. Rationale: influences the
  app, reusable, and wine-region tourists clear $50 without blinking (tastings start
  there). Premium print spec (unit cost assumption $8–15 at ~1k units, to validate).
- **Kickstarter noted** as a consumer-demand validation channel (indie tarot decks
  are a reliably-funded category) — separate experiment from winery validation.
- **Sequencing (Ed's own call):** the Sonoma deck comes *after* something in the app
  is generating sales revenue and usage. → Idea 1 is not first in the build order;
  the interview's next thread is what that in-app revenue is.

**Status:** captured + pressure-tested; costing deferred to plan phase.

## Idea 2 — App subscription (the whisper is the flagship)

**The whisper (as told):** the first-screen "Whisper" grows into an input that clamps
the draw — say "I'm eating fish tonight" and only cards with fish-pairing matches can
be pulled; ask about your love life and the card pulled has resonant connection
("laser-guided reading"). Then photo inputs: **photograph a restaurant wine list** →
only cards matching wines on the list can be drawn; grand finale, **photograph a wall
of wine in a shop** → whisper ingests the shelf, the draw points at the bottle to
grab. Kills decision paralysis; "no more scanning every bottle until you give up and
pick cabernet sauvignon." Ed: the text whisper itself probably isn't monetizable —
it's the new modality; the photo tiers are the paid product.

**Subscription contents (Ed's candidates):** photo-whisper (list scan, shelf scan);
memory journal beyond a free cap (10 or 22); minor arcana (free tier = majors only —
note: all 78 exist in the app today); cellar beyond a few bottles; **multi-card
spreads** (3-card pulls with a combined, custom-feeling reading — "almost endless
depth"); **personalized recommendation engine** that learns your palate and grows
with you (roadmap-tier, premium-feeling).

**Gut price:** ~$5/mo.

**Economics note (flag for engineering, not re-derived here):** photo-whisper breaks
the cached-enrichment model — a wine list or shelf photo is a per-USE vision call
(every list/shelf is unique), not a once-ever per-wine cost. Assumption to validate
with the engineering session: $0.02–0.10 per scan. Alignment worth keeping: the free
tier is exactly the $0-marginal-cost surface (draws), the paid tier is exactly the
features with real COGS (scans, spread readings, cellar enrichment).

**Decisions (Jul 15):**
- **CORE TENET (ratified): gate utility, never sentiment.** Memory journal free and
  unlimited forever. Cellar generously free (mapping/`IN YOUR CELLAR` works on a
  real-sized rack — "how they invest without investing"); depth (full stories,
  pairing detail) is what's gated.
- **Paid tier:** photo-whisper (list + shelf scans), minor arcana, multi-card
  spreads, cellar depth, personalization engine (roadmap).
- **One free taste of every scan type** (shelf, list, maybe single-bottle), gated
  after first use — everyone feels the grand finale once.
- **Pricing: $5/mo, $40/yr** (annual preferred framing).
- Honest scale math (for the plan): 2–5% freemium conversion → subscription becomes
  a bank-relevant line north of ~20k users. It's the scale engine, not the first
  dollar.

**Status:** captured + pressure-tested; costing deferred to plan phase (shelf-scan
per-use cost owed by engineering).

## Idea 3 — The card-drawn wine club (quarterly)

**As told (Jul 15):** quarterly, **$160–200/quarter, ~6 bottles**. A card is drawn
(possibly live-streamed, possibly a bigger spread — Celtic cross / 3×3 — for denser
theming); the next box is built around it. Each bottle ships with its arcana guide so
members experience the card's lenses without performing the ritual — a shared,
simultaneous experience across the club. Community layer: members weigh in on what
resonated, vote on future directions ("more rosés or finally the Jura"). Quarterly
chosen deliberately — monthly is "a perpetual mad dash" and quarterly leaves time to
line up deals. Ed assumed a Patreon on top and a future sales/distribution partner.

**Legal architecture (confirmed via CA ABC's third-party-provider framework):** Ed
stays unlicensed as a *curator/marketer*; a licensed retailer is merchant of record —
they control sale acceptance, fulfillment, shipping, and the funds; Ed's revenue is a
curation/marketing/content fee. Fee structure (flat vs. %-of-sales) is exactly what
the alcohol attorney must bless. Shipping fulfillment warehouses in CA need a Public
Warehouse License (partner's problem, not Ed's).

**Routes (in ascending complexity):**
1. **Local pickup club v1** — partner SF wine bar/shop is the licensee; members
   collect the box at a quarterly *pickup party* (which is also the event/content
   engine). No interstate shipping maze. Ed already knows SF wine-bar owners.
2. **Retail partner ships** — a shop with off-sale + direct-shipper permits fulfills;
   state-by-state shipping patchwork is their compliance burden.
3. **White-label club platform** — turnkey branded-club operators exist (no license,
   no inventory, no logistics for the brand owner); trade margin for zero ops.

**Economics sketch (assumptions explicit, plan-phase to refine):** $180/quarter box;
partner needs ~30–35% wine margin; shipping $20–30/box (argues for pickup v1); Ed's
curation+content fee ~15–20% → **~$27–36/box, ~$110–145/yr/member** — a club member
is worth ~3× a $40/yr subscriber. 100 members ≈ $72k/yr gross through the partner,
Ed's ~$12–14k. Patreon-the-platform dropped: the club IS the patronage; voting,
live pulls, and community are club benefits, not a second subscription.

**Decisions (Jul 15–16):**
- **Pickup-party v1 ratified** — starts with friends, doubles as the probe for
  whether launch events are a real channel.
- **SF benchmark (Jul 2026):** shop clubs run ~$40–70/mo for 2–4 bottles (Arlequin
  $69/mo; Ferry Plaza $40–68/mo; Oxbow ~$40/mo; SFWTC $60–150/mo). Ed's
  $180–200/quarter for 6 ≈ $60–67/mo-equivalent — **at market, not above it**. The
  cut grows via structure, not price.
- **Cut structure (proposed, Ed to ratify):** split billing — the wine box at the
  partner's normal retail (theirs), plus a **membership fee that is 100% Ed's**
  (content, community, live pull, pickup-party access; media/experience, not
  alcohol — cleaner under the ABC framework too). Plus an in-box quarterly
  **collectible card** (printed, cheap, ties the deck ecosystem, makes the box
  incomparable to a plain shop club). Premium tier (aspirational/deep-cut box) later.
- **Honest v1 framing:** at 30–50 members the club is proof + content + community,
  not income (~$1–2k/quarter to Ed regardless of structure).
- **Bar owners re-weighted: effectively cold** ("wouldn't recognize me walked in off
  the street"). Outreach plan = event-first: become a paying regular for a month;
  pitch a slow-night ticketed ritual evening with a guaranteed bar minimum and Ed
  bringing the crowd (friends list + fake-door emails); the club is pitched only
  after 2–3 successful nights. Odds honest: high for one filled Tuesday, unknown for
  the club — which is why the event is the ask.

**Status:** captured + pressure-tested; costing deferred to plan phase.

## Idea 4 — Buy button in The Pour (affiliate v1 → licensed later)

**As told (Jul 16):** buy button on The Pour's bottle page → opens the bottle at a
partner retailer (hypothetically wine.com) as an affiliate link. Not literally one
tap, but the app already shows details/price/member deals. Later, if it takes off,
bring it in-house ("everyone that's licensed starts without a license"). Alternate
flavor: partner with wine-searcher so it feels like digging the crates rather than
one endorsed retailer.

**Commission reality (verified Jul 2026):** wine.com runs ~4–6% on new-customer
sales, 1–2.5% repeat (via Rakuten). On a $30 bottle ≈ **$1.20–1.80 per new-customer
order**. A rounding line until large scale — but it monetizes free users at zero
marginal cost and, more valuably, **measures purchase-intent conversion**, the single
number that makes wineries, club partners, and banks believe the mechanism works.

**Pressure points:** (1) availability matching is the real product problem — the
revealed bottle must actually be in the partner's stock (LWIN identity → retailer SKU
mapping; fallback behavior when out of stock); engineering owns feasibility. (2)
Philosophy fit: one endorsed partner = "trust the spirits" consistent; wine-searcher
price-comparison = Vivino-adjacent transparency — usable as a deep-cut/power feature,
not the default. (3) FTC affiliate disclosure required in-app. (4) Same ABC
third-party cloud (percentage commissions) — on the attorney's list.

**Status:** captured; v1 = single endorsed affiliate partner; licensure = distant
option, not planned.

## Idea 5 — Michelin-restaurant deck (food × wine marriage)

**As told (Jul 16):** partner with Michelin-star restaurants to build a special deck
on their wine lists — every card/lens recommends a wine from those lists and talks
about the dishes it pairs with that the restaurant is known for. "A huge
advertisement for the restaurants that people actually want to see" — precedent: the
fashionable-cookbook economy, French Laundry coffee lines. These kitchens already
license their brand into lifestyle objects.

**Sharpened:** same Michelin-model family as Idea 1 (editorial award → partner
activation), but a new buyer class (prestige restaurants) and plausibly
**digital-first** (an in-app premium deck needs no print run; physical follows if it
earns it). SF's Michelin density = the geography advantage again. Sales reality:
prestige kitchens are credibility-guarded — this sequences *after* a sold-through
Sonoma edition gives Ed a proof object. Restaurant names/likeness require signed
partnership; their PR upside (cookbook-adjacent object) is the pitch.

**Status:** captured; sequenced late (needs the Sonoma proof first).

## Idea 6 — In-app cosmetic decks & the passive layer

**As told (Jul 16):** sell decks in the app with different artwork/aesthetics — reskin
to fit your vibe. Part of a general want: **passive revenue that scales with users,
no production/shipping/partnership weight.**

**Sharpened:** one-time IAP skins, high-margin, the truest passive line. Unit math to
validate: commissioned art per skin ~$3–8k (AI-assist ruled out for sold art by Ed's
own standard — does the standard soften for $5 digital skins? Ed's call, flag
raised); at $5–10 a skin needs ~500–1,500 buyers to clear the commission. **Artist
rev-share flips the risk**: artists bring their own audiences, Ed fronts $0 —
digital editions become the test bed for which art earns a physical print run.
Skins are also gacha-native (seasonal drops, collector psychology).

**Related decisions:**
- **Spirit tone packs (bubbly fortune-teller etc.): PARKED with a red flag.** Not on
  taste (Ed's own "cheesy?" instinct) but on unit economics: every tone = rewriting
  the entire hand-approved corpus (78 cards × lenses × guidebook) in a new locked
  voice. The single voice is the moat AND the cost ceiling. Revisit only if content
  generation gets an order of magnitude cheaper without losing hand-approval.
- **Sponsored slot below the curated three (PROPOSED AMENDMENT to the core tenet,
  awaiting Ed's ratification):** at scale (~50k+ users), a clearly-labeled sponsored
  slot may appear *after* the three editorial tiers — never inside the reading,
  never replacing an editorial pick, always FTC-labeled. The tenet stays: the
  spirits' own picks are never for sale; the ad shelf below them can be.

**Decisions (Jul 16):**
- **Artist rev-share RATIFIED** as the skin model; a digital deck that performs earns
  elevation to a physical print run.
- **AI-art stance (Ed):** not if it looks AI-generated; wants artists paid and
  supported; won't say "never" (some artists integrate AI without slop) but avoids
  it. Wants a costing of self-producing a founder deck one card at a time, possibly
  phased out later once real artists are aboard.
- **Sponsored-slot amendment RATIFIED** — labeled slot below the three editorial
  tiers, at scale only, never inside the reading.
- **Spirit-tone red flag AGREED.**

- **TWO-TIER DECK LAW (Ed, Jul 16):** every deck product — digital or print — is
  explicitly a **Majors Edition (22)** or **Full Arcana (78)**. Reason: subscribers
  who've unlocked the minors will expect purchased decks to cover all 78 unless the
  deck is clearly labeled 22-only. Costing consequences: commissioned art 22-card
  ≈ $5.5–10k vs 78-card ≈ $20–35k (assumption $250–450/card); rev-share digital 22
  is the standard artist offer (78 = prestige option for proven sellers); founder
  deck at 78 = 150–300 Ed-hours (reinforces free-default-skin verdict); print unit
  cost 78 ≈ $10–18 vs 22 ≈ $8–15. Sonoma stays a Majors Edition; its in-app skin
  covers 22 and minors render in default art — labeled, expected, honest.
- **Google clearance (Jul 16): submitted.** Informal guidance: no copyright claims
  asserted over card games, digital or print. Formal clearance expected ≤3 weeks.
  Risk downgraded from open to pending-paperwork.

**Status:** captured + decided; founder-deck costing in phase-2 notes.

## Phase 2 findings (market + validation)

- **Beachhead signal (Ed, Jul 16):** the tarot-curious lean in hardest — one friend
  draws a card regularly *for the readings alone, no wine*. Wine-intense friends not

  wine-conversion bridge (pour views, affiliate taps) as THE open question — the

  or the cohort's retention data is lost (flagged to engineering as a task chip).
- **Cohort design note:** tag users by affinity (wine / tarot / neither) at
  onboarding or behaviorally, so retention and conversion can be read per segment —
  the segmentation the whole plan leans on.
