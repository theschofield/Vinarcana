# Vintner's Arcana — The Invitation Prompt (Approach hero lines)

The big DM Serif line under the deck on The Approach. One appears per visit, cycling at random
(no repeats until the set is exhausted).

## The approved set (canon)
1. Go on. We don't bite.
2. Fate favors the thirsty.
3. The cards have been talking about you.
4. Curiosity looks good on you.
5. It already knows. Go ahead, ask.
6. Ask nothing. Learn everything.
7. Shall we?
8. The spirits have opinions tonight.

## The generation prompt (paste into any session to extend the set)

> Write N more "invitation lines" for a tarot-and-wine app's opening screen. The line sits under a
> face-down deck, spoken BY the deck's spirit TO the visitor, before any card is drawn.
>
> **What it is:** a dare disguised as hospitality. The spirit already knows which card you'll get
> and what it will mean; the line is the smile before the trick. It is NOT an instruction (no
> "tap", "draw", "pick"), NOT a description of the app, and NOT fortune-cookie wisdom.
>
> **Register:** sly, confident, warm, a little dangerous. The four moods that work (write across
> all of them): the playful dare ("Go on. We don't bite."), the sly prophecy ("Fate favors the
> thirsty."), delicious surveillance ("The cards have been talking about you."), and pure elegance
> ("Shall we?"). Flattery only by invitation ("Curiosity looks good on you.").
>
> **Form:** 2–7 words per sentence, max ~40 characters total (it renders large). One or two
> sentences; if two, the second turns or answers the first ("It already knows. Go ahead, ask.").
> Wine may be alluded to (thirst, a pour) but never named directly — no "wine," "bottle," "glass."
>
> **Hard bans:** em dashes; "it's not X, it's Y" pivots; rhetorical question + retort; staccato
> fragment triplets; anything an app would say ("get started", "your reading awaits"); anything
> spooky-halloween ("if you dare", "enter the darkness"); naming the wonder ("magic", "mystical").
>
> **The test:** read it aloud in a low voice with one eyebrow raised. If it doesn't survive that
> delivery, cut it. If it could appear on a landing page for any app, cut it. If the spirit sounds
> eager instead of certain, cut it.
